# When to Call 118

## Call 118 (Emergency Services) When:

### Life-Threatening Situations (Code RED)
- Cardiac arrest or no pulse
- Severe breathing difficulty or cessation
- Unconsciousness or unresponsiveness
- Massive bleeding that cannot be controlled
- Severe trauma (car accident, fall from height, gunshot)
- Suspected stroke (FAST: Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call)
- Severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis)
- Poisoning or overdose

### When NOT to Call 118
- Minor cuts and bruises — go to ED or guardia medica
- Common cold or flu symptoms — contact your GP
- Prescription renewals — go to the Prescription Office
- Chronic condition follow-up — book a specialist appointment
- Certificate requests — go to the Certification Office

## What to Tell the 118 Operator
1. Your exact location (address, floor, room if indoors)
2. What happened (brief description)
3. Number of people involved
4. Condition of the person(s) (conscious, breathing, bleeding)
5. Your phone number
6. Do NOT hang up until the operator tells you to

## While Waiting for the Ambulance
- Stay on the phone if the operator asks you to
- Do NOT move the injured person (unless in immediate danger)
- Apply pressure to bleeding wounds
- If unconscious but breathing, place in recovery position
- Do NOT give food or water
- Unlock doors and clear a path for paramedics

## 118 Contact
- Emergency number: **118**
- Free from any phone, including mobile phones without credit
- Available 24/7, 365 days a year
